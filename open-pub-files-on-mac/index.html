How to open .pub files on a Mac (2026): every option compared

There has never been a Mac version of Microsoft Publisher, and after October 2026 there will not be a Windows one either. Here is every realistic way to open a .pub file on a Mac today, what each costs, and what each one gets wrong. We make one of these tools, and we say so where it matters.

Short version

  • Just need to look at it, one page, right now: LibreOffice (free) or PubHarbor (free). Both are fine for single-page flyers.
  • Multi-page newsletter or bulletin: every free and cheap option on this list shares the same parser bug and will jumble the text across pages. See the multi-page problem. Check the output carefully.
  • Need to keep editing it: you need IDML (for Affinity or InDesign). Today that means Markzware at agency prices; or PubFile at $29.99 after three free conversions on your own files.
  • Sensitive content (member lists, pupils' names): do not use an online converter.

The multi-page problem, explained once

LibreOffice, PubHarbor and PUB Reader all read .pub files with libmspub, an open-source library reverse-engineered from the format by the LibreOffice project. It is a genuine achievement and it handles single-page documents reasonably. But Publisher newsletters use linked text boxes: a story starts in a box on page 1 and continues in a box on page 2 or 3. libmspub has never implemented the link. It fills every linked box with the whole story. Result: pages 1-3 each contain the full article stacked on top of whatever else is on the page, and the last page is blank.

That is LibreOffice bug 67299, "text is repeated on all pages", open since 2013. Any app on stock libmspub inherits it. It is also why reviews of the paid Mac apps say things like "stacks words from other pages" and "only exports one page".

How to tell if a document is affected: it has more than one page and body text that continues from one page to the next. Flyers, posters, certificates and business cards are usually fine. Newsletters, bulletins, booklets and programmes usually are not.

LibreOffice Draw (free)

LibreOffice opens .pub files in its Draw module: File → Open, pick the .pub file. You can then export to PDF (File → Export As → PDF), or edit the objects as a drawing.

From the Terminal you can also convert in bulk without opening the app:

/Applications/LibreOffice.app/Contents/MacOS/soffice --headless --convert-to pdf:draw_pdf_Export *.pub

Good

free, private, runs on any Mac, scriptable.

Not so good

the multi-page bug above; font substitution with no warning; the result is a Draw document, not a page-layout document, so you cannot get it into Affinity or InDesign as editable frames. Microsoft's own community answers recommend it with the caveat that editing "may not always be as precise as you may like".

PubHarbor (free, in-app purchases)

PubHarbor (Eutopix Labs, 2026) is a viewer for Mac, iPhone and iPad with Quick Look, thumbnails, text search, and PDF/PNG export; batch export on Mac. Files never leave the device. Its site is candid: "a viewer and archive converter, not a Publisher editor", "conversion is best effort".

We tested it on the file from bug 67299. Its own compatibility note reports "Rendered locally with libmspub 0.1.5", i.e. the stock library, and the exported PDF had the full story repeated on pages 1-3 with page 4 empty, while the compatibility report showed zero serious warnings. It also has no editable (IDML/DOCX) output.

Good

free, private, iPhone/iPad, polished viewer.

Not so good

multi-page bug; report does not catch it; PDF and images only.

PUB Reader : for MS Publisher ($29.99)

PUB Reader (RootRise Technologies) has been on the Mac App Store since 2015 and is still updated (v4.0, January 2026). It opens .pub files and exports PDF and images. The same developer sells PUB Converter for $39.99.

At the time of writing it has 3.9 stars from 109 ratings. The one-star reviews cluster on two things: multi-page documents ("stacks words from other pages", "only exports one page", bug 67299 again, still present in v4.0 according to a December 2025 review) and the lack of a trial ("how do I get a refund"). The five-star reviews are from people with single-page files who were relieved anything opened them at all.

Good

long track record, private, straightforward.

Not so good

$29.99 with no trial for something LibreOffice does free with the same defects; view/PDF only.

Online converters (free to $5 per file)

Searching "pub to pdf" turns up a dozen upload sites. Some run LibreOffice on a server (same bug); a few run actual Publisher on Windows and produce faithful PDFs. Either way you are handing the document to a third party. For a public flyer that is fine. For a parish directory, a school newsletter with children's names, or anything with addresses, it is not. Check the site's retention policy before uploading; many do not have one.

Markzware OmniMarkz ($469 per year)

Markzware has converted desktop-publishing formats since 1993 and is the established professional answer. OmniMarkz (macOS) previews and converts InDesign, QuarkXPress, PDF and, via their Publisher-migration path, .pub files to IDML, with a file inventory. It uses Markzware's own engine, not libmspub. There is a preview-only free download. They also offer a per-file conversion service: $39 for files under 20 MB, up to $199 for very large ones, done by a technician within hours.

Good

mature, editable IDML output, handles many formats.

Not so good

$469/year is agency pricing; the per-file service means uploading and paying per document, which adds up for a folder of bulletins.

PubFile (ours; free for three files, $29.99 one-time)

PubFile is a Mac App Store app (1.0, October 2026), built specifically for the multi-page problem and for editable output. It opens and previews .pub files (with Quick Look), converts to IDML and PDF, and writes a fidelity report per file that checks the placed text against the original page by page. The free version converts three files at full quality so you can judge it on your own documents. Full details

Good

multi-page text flow fixed; IDML for Affinity (free) and InDesign; report that says what broke; nothing uploaded; try before buying.

Not so good

Mac App Store only; no iPhone/iPad version; no DOCX output at launch; some Publisher features (WordArt, embedded objects) will still need manual work, the report will say which.

What to move to once the file is open

If you want to keep editing your documents, the destination in 2026 is Affinity, which is free and imports IDML. See Publisher alternatives for churches and schools for the full reasoning, including where Word, Pages and Canva fit.
